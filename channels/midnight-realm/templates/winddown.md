# Wind-down (~5 minutes, ~550 words)

The last section. The pace slows: shorter sentences, longer pauses (paragraph breaks),
fewer names, and no new facts that need attention.

1. **Close the journey.** One sentence that retraces the route, naming the same two or
   three places the opening promised, in the order they were visited. Nothing else is
   summarized.
2. **Meet them wherever they are.** They may be nearly asleep, or already asleep with
   these words becoming part of a dream, or wide awake. Say so in one sentence, without
   asking anything of them.
3. **What the story was about.** Two or three short paragraphs on what tonight's story
   says about people — ambition, grief, loyalty, the price of wanting something. Drawn
   from what was actually told, never from lore that was not in the video. No new names.
4. **Their own world.** Turn it toward the listener, plainly: name the ordinary things
   waiting for them tomorrow in the language of this game's dangers, and say those can
   be faced. Three or four sentences, warm, never advice.
5. **Body.** Breath getting longer, the room getting heavier and warmer. Two or three
   paragraphs, each shorter than the last.
6. **Blessing.** Three or four sentences that begin the same way — a wish for rest,
   for quieter dreams, for waking well — built from this game's own vocabulary. This is
   the emotional peak of the ending and it is spoken slowest.
7. **Thanks.** One sentence for spending the night with "{channel_name}".
8. **Farewell.** Close with "{farewell}". It is the last line of the video, addressed to
   "{address}", and nothing comes after it.
