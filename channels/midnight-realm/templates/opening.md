# Opening ritual (~3 minutes, ~360 words)

Speak to a real person lying in a real bed, in their own world. Rewrite every sentence in
fresh words for this game: the example lines below show tone only and must never appear
in the script.

## The entrance (varies per video)

The first thirty seconds change from video to video, so a viewer who watches three of
them does not hear the same welcome three times. One mode is chosen for tonight and named
at the end of this brief; write that one and ignore the others.

**welcome** — "Good evening, and welcome to {channel_name}." Then ask where they are
listening from tonight, and guess at two ordinary situations they might be in right now:
a dark bedroom, weather at the window, a long day behind them.

**question** — No greeting at all. Open on the question itself, addressed straight to
them, and let the two guesses follow. "{channel_name}" is named once, later, in passing,
as the place this is happening rather than as an announcement.

**cold open** — Open on one small, concrete thing from this game that the listener's
hands remember: a sound, an object, a gesture they repeated a thousand times without
thinking. Three or four sentences of it, close and sensory. Only then turn to them and
their room, and name "{channel_name}" as you do.

**arrival** — Open by setting them down somewhere quiet inside this game's world, built
from {welcome_image}: a shelter, a light, weather outside that cannot reach them. Three
sentences. Then step back out of it — that place is in the story, and they are here, in
their own bed, with "{channel_name}" — and carry on.

## Then, in this order, whichever entrance was used

1. **Welcome and settle.** They are welcome here whatever the answer. One breath, one
   instruction to settle in, one line letting the world outside fall away.
2. **Name tonight's story, once.** Say the game and tonight's scope ({scope_line}) in a
   single sentence. Any praise is for the story, never for the video.
3. **Why this story is hard to hold.** One or two sentences on why this lore is scattered
   or buried — item text, ruins, things the game never says outright. This is why the
   video exists; never claim credit for assembling it.
4. **No homework.** There is nothing to remember and nothing to follow closely. Drifting
   off halfway is a good outcome, and so is staying awake. Say both.
5. **The itinerary.** One sentence that names, in order, the three or four places the
   night will travel through, and one more naming the kinds of people met along the way.
   No twists, no endings, no names that need explaining.
6. **Body cue.** Eyes may close. Shoulders drop. One or two short sentences.
7. **Themes, not plot.** Three clauses, each beginning the same way, naming what the
   story is *about*: an image, a promise that turns, a price that is paid. Built from
   this game's own vocabulary.
8. **Straight in.** End on the sentence before the first chapter. No pause, no summary,
   no "let's begin": the chapter announcement follows immediately and is written as part
   of the next section, addressing the listener as "{address}" if it addresses them at
   all.

Example of tone only (do not reuse):
> Wherever you are tonight, and however long the day was, you're welcome here.
